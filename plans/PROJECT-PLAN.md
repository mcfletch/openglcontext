| [EXAMINE-NAVIGATION.md](EXAMINE-NAVIGATION.md) | ✅ Landed | **A right-drag threw the camera off into space and left the horizon on its side.** Three causes, all measured: the drag was scaled by the distance from where it *started* to the edge of the window, a different divisor on each side, so a 30-pixel drag beginning 40 pixels from the edge of an 800-pixel window turned **135°** against **7°** for the same movement the other way; the trackball rotated about the camera's own two axes, which compose into roll, so 200 pixels of diagonal drag left the up vector at `(-0.87, -0.50, 0.00)`; and nothing bounded the pitch, with no dolly and no pan to recover with. | [WATER-GERSTNER-CRESTS.md](WATER-GERSTNER-CRESTS.md) | 📋 Planned | **`choppy` is not choppy.** It is `flowing` with the numbers turned up, because the field under all three styles is a sum of sines and a sum of sines is symmetric about its mean: measured over a 60x60 m patch the height field skews -0.006 for `flowing` and +0.019 for `choppy`, where a real short-crested sea runs around +0.3 to +0.6. That asymmetry -- narrow crests, broad flat troughs -- is what the eye reads as rough water, so turning the amplitude up gives a bigger swell rather than a choppier one. Gerstner displacement moves the surface sideways toward the crest as well as up, which piles water into it. It costs the height-field contract [WATER.md](WATER.md) promised to buoyancy, so `wave_point` meshes and a new `surface_height_at` inverts the displacement for anything that floats. |
| [WATER-FOAM.md](WATER-FOAM.md) | 📋 Planned | **There is no foam in the engine** -- `foam`, `froth`, `whitecap`, `breaker` and `spray` appear in no Python module and no shader. Whitecaps are how an eye judges wind on water, so a lake that heaves without ever breaking white reads as something viscous. Foam as a scalar field computed from the wave field rather than painted: the Gerstner fold for crests that are actually overturning, surface steepness for streaks down a wave face, and shallow depth for the band along a beach. What it does to the surface matters as much as where it goes -- albedo toward a dirty white, roughness up hard, and the dielectric specular down, since foam is air and film and leaving water's F0 on it is what makes rendered foam look like plastic. |
| [WATER-SHOALING.md](WATER-SHOALING.md) | 📋 Planned | **The wave field never sees the bed.** `wave_height(style, x, z, when)` is a pure function of position, time and style; the only depth in the package is the box a `Volume` occupies and the transparency that shows a pale bed through the shallows. So the same swell that crosses open water runs up a beach at the same height, wavelength and direction and stops at the waterline rather than breaking on it. A `DepthField` the wave function can ask, then linear wave theory per point: crests shorten and stand taller as the bed rises, turn toward the depth contour so they break along the beach, and clamp at `H/d > 0.78` with the excess handed to foam. The dispersion relation is one curve per style, sampled into a table, so the per-point cost is a lookup. Deep water stays bit-identical, which is what makes the rest safe. |

<a name="rebinding-a-node-s-children-notifies-nobody"></a>
### Rebinding a node's `children` notifies nobody — ⬜ Todo

`node.children = [child]` is the natural way to write a scenegraph change and it **silently fails to reach the renderer**. `FlatPass` walks the scenegraph once (`SGObserver`) and thereafter keeps its set of renderable paths current from the `OList.NEW_CHILD_EVT` / `DEL_CHILD_EVT` signals a node's `children` list sends as it is added to and removed from. Assigning a *new* list to the field replaces the observed one instead of mutating it, so no signal is sent and the pass goes on drawing whatever it last integrated: the scenegraph is correct, the screen is stale, and nothing anywhere reports a problem. `children[:] = [child]` is the spelling that works, which is a distinction no caller should have to know. **Confirmed in the wild** — it is what made twig-bb draw every weapon with the model of whichever one was held when the level loaded (that project's B32), invisible until somebody switched weapon at *runtime*, since a scene integrated after the change looks right. **Three call sites in this package have the same shape**: `physics/debugdraw.py` rebinds `_proxy_group.children` when new proxies appear, `scenegraph/tilesterrain.py` rebinds `self.children` as tiles stream in, and `bin/gltf_demo.py` rebinds a layer's — each drawing a stale set until something else happens to re-integrate. Wanted: fix it **where the field is set**, so the obvious spelling is the correct one — an `MFNode` set that mutates the existing `OList` in place (and so emits add/remove per element) rather than binding a new list, in pyvrml97's field machinery. That is shared 28-year-old code, so it wants its own Red/Green pass over what currently depends on identity-of-list rather than contents, plus a decision on whether the same rule should hold for every `MF*` field or only for the node-valued ones. The **other half of this defect is already fixed** (2026-08-03): `NodePath.invalidate` was marking only children and grandchildren broken because `iterdescendents` walked two levels against its own docstring, so a detached subtree deeper than that stayed in the draw set; `iterdescendents` now walks every generation and pyvrml97's own test, which had pinned the two-level behaviour, is corrected. Walkable triangles by slope and facing; neighbours by shared edge, **welded by position** because a collision mesh from a level is a triangle soup where the same corner has a different index in every triangle that touches it — matching indices joins nothing and leaves every cell an island; A* over the cells and a **string-pulled** path through the portals, because a path along cell centres zigzags across an empty room and every corner it rounds is one it did not need. 25 tests, no GL: an open floor gives a straight line, an obstacle is routed round, a ramp is climbed, an island is unreachable. **On `oa_dm1` it builds 1220 connected cells in 0.03 s and then fails at the last step — no spawn point resolves to a cell** (`cell_at`, and the coordinates line up, so it is the point-in-triangle test or the slope filter). The headroom test is too blunt for a real level and is off by default: it compares bounding boxes where it wants a distance, and removed 1092 of 1220 cells almost none of which were near a wall. No off-mesh links yet, so jump pads are not edges. Next steps are in twig-bb's PROJECT-PLAN §3b T2

**2026-08-29 — the route is now the route a person would take.** The demo took a
visibly wrong line: out of the left room, straight at the far wall, then up the
wall to find the gap. Three things were wrong, and all three are engine defects
rather than demo ones.

- **The funnel worked out each portal's left and right end from its own moving
  apex.** Left and right are a fact about the direction a portal is *crossed*,
  not about where the walker currently stands, so a portal reversed whenever the
  apex passed to the other side of it and the funnel narrowed the wrong side of
  itself. On an open floor a route across the grain of the triangulation gained
  a corner behind the start; handed a corridor that doubled back, it returned a
  line straight through the walls. Portals are now oriented once, per direction,
  in `_join`, and both the funnel and the new raycast read them as stored.
- **A\* costed a step between cell centres**, which over a grid of triangles is
  a Manhattan metric: a staircase costs the same as the two sides it climbs, so
  every monotone corridor tied and the winner was whichever the queue reached
  first. Since the funnel cannot leave the corridor it is handed, that tie was
  the route. A step is now costed from where the walker stands to the **nearest
  point on the portal it leaves by**, with the search state carrying the portal
  a cell was entered through; the diagonal is then strictly cheaper, which is
  what it is. Portal *midpoints* were tried first and are not enough — they are
  exact along the mesh's own diagonal and √2 long across it.
- **Nothing removed a corner the corridor rather than the geometry put there.**
  `NavMesh.visible()` walks the mesh along a line and answers whether an
  unbroken run of cells covers it — a public API a bot wants anyway — and the
  pulled line now keeps only the furthest corner still in sight of the last.

On the demo's geometry the main route falls from 37.30 m to 33.34 m, which is
the taut line: six points, four of them the ends of the two walls. `path()` also
went from 22 ms to 2.5 ms on that mesh — `cell_at` now narrows by footprint box
before testing cells, `_height_at` reads a precomputed normal, and the funnel
and search run on plain floats rather than allocating numpy arrays per portal.
`NavMesh.corridor()` is public, so the demo draws the engine's own search
instead of the copy of A\* it used to carry. 43 tests.

### Animated PBR surfaces — ✅ Complete

A **surface deformer** on `PBRMesh`: a per-frame callable a *material* uses to move the geometry it is painted on, as distinct from morph targets and skinning, which are the mesh's own animation. It rides the same pipeline — rest arrays, the morph→skin→surface chain, the version counter, the dynamic VBO re-upload — and adds the two properties that make it safe: the deformer always sees the **rest pose** (deforming its own last output compounds, and a one-unit wave walks the surface away in seconds) and is handed **copies** (so a deformer that writes in place cannot corrupt what it is given next frame). Texture coordinates join the dynamic buffers **only** when the mesh says its UVs move (`deforms_texcoords`), so a skinned character does not pay to re-upload UVs that never change. Together with the `uv_transform`/`texCoordMask` and material-version seams that already existed for `KHR_texture_transform`, this is what lets Quake 3 `.shader` animation — `tcMod`, `rgbGen`, `alphaGen`, `animMap`, `deformVertexes` — be drawn: see [twig-bb](../../twig-bb/README.md#surfaces-that-move), whose `animator.py` is the worked example. **Red/Green TDD: 22 tests**, no GL. Docs: [docs/pbr.html#animated-surfaces](../docs/pbr.html)

<a name="spatial-audio"></a>
### [SPATIAL-AUDIO.md](SPATIAL-AUDIO.md) — ✅ Complete

Sound placed **in** the scene, on glTF's `KHR_audio_emitter` model (the Web Audio `PannerNode` model) rather than a private format — the same decision physics made with OMI, so a scene authored in Blender or Godot arrives with its sound intact. The engine ships as the standalone **`omi_audio`** package (model/spatial/clip/synth/mixer/device/engine — numpy only, no renderer in it); OpenGLContext keeps the integrations: `scenegraph/audio.py` (`AudioEmitter`/`AudioSource`, and VRML97's long-declared `Sound`/`AudioClip` finally audible on their own ellipsoid curve) and `OpenGLContext/audio/` (scene/settings). Three distance models, a Web Audio cone, equal-power panning about the camera-as-listener, and an underwater low-pass. The mixer is **ours, in numpy**, so no copyleft audio library is a dependency: a fixed voice pool with priority stealing, per-block gain ramping, and a hard no-allocation/no-lock/no-log rule on the audio thread (asserted by a tracemalloc test). `play()` hands out a generation-checked handle so a stolen voice's old owner steers nothing rather than somebody else's explosion. **Silence is a backend**: package absent, device won't open, or the backend's own null output — one warning and a `NullDevice`, and `open_device()` cannot raise. Sound is driven from `FlatPass.beginFrame` (not `Render`, which is overridden per profile) and costs nothing until a scene has an audible node. `miniaudio` is the only audio dependency and is **optional** because it ships no `manylinux_aarch64` wheel. Player settings live in a `ContextDefinition.audio` sub-node and generate their own settings section; the application's `master_gain` multiplies with the player's `volume` rather than being overwritten by it. **Red/Green TDD: 336 tests**, split with the code (`omi_audio`'s own suite plus OpenGLContext's `test_audio_{nodes,scene,gltf,pass_wiring,demo_audible}`), including two written against defects found only by listening — one asserting the level in dBFS at the *device boundary* for a source at a stated position, one holding the demo's own scene to a comfortable band. Docs: [docs/audio.html](../docs/audio.html) with data-flow diagrams; demo `tests/audio_spatial.py`

<a name="particle-effects"></a>
### [PARTICLE-EFFECTS.md](PARTICLE-EFFECTS.md) — ✅ Complete

Fire, smoke, sparks, explosions, impacts and trails as camera-facing quads — **one instanced draw per system**, however many particles. `scenegraph/particles.py`: a `ParticlePool` of numpy arrays with **the living packed at the front** (survivors compact over the dead, so the live set is one contiguous slice and the upload is one `glBufferSubData`), and a `ParticleEmitter` rendering node with declared fields and `UI_HINTS`. **Per particle is state, per system is a curve**: seven floats per instance, with colour and size over life as two uniforms the shader lerps — so editing `size` resizes what is already in the air. Billboarding in eye space (exact at any camera orientation, no per-particle matrix); the node picks view vs model-view so the shader has no branch. Depth tested, depth writes off, additive by default, and a soft round dot computed in the fragment shader when no texture is given — so an effect needs no asset. Six presets. Careful about the things that bite: exponential drag (linear reverses on a long frame), a clamped frame time, a fractional emission accumulator, and a cone that samples the spherical cap rather than the angle. **Red/Green TDD: 71 tests**, no GL, including an allocation assertion. Docs: [docs/particles.html](../docs/particles.html); demo `tests/particles_effects.py`

<a name="fog-and-swimming"></a>
### Fog, and swimming — ✅ Complete

Two nodes that had been declared and never driven, finally driven. **VRML97's `Fog`** (`scenegraph/fog.py`) is rendered through the PBR shader: the pass has collected `nodetypes.Fog` paths for years and nothing read them, and a dangling `context.gltf_fog` seam that nothing ever set is retired in its favour. A node rather than a number *because fog is a property of a place* — it binds and stacks like a viewpoint, so being under water, in smoke and in clear air are three fogs in one scene with one in force. **Both of the specification's curves**, because they are not approximations of each other: `LINEAR` fades from the first metre, `EXPONENTIAL` hangs back and closes in, and the difference is whether the weapon in the player's hands is tinted as much as the far wall — which is exactly what separates *being inside a medium* from a coloured pane laid over the screen. One uniform (the reciprocal of the visible range) serves every mode, the blend happens in linear HDR before tone mapping, and a scene with no fog stays pixel-identical. **Swimming** stops being a use of noclip: `MovementMode.applyTo(platform)` is where a mode puts the *body* into the state its movement assumes, `SwimMode` asks for `set_swim(buoyancy=...)` rather than `set_fly(True)`, and omi_physics' `CharacterController` grew a swim step — flying's movement, walking's collision, and a vertical that is neither: gravity scaled by what buoyancy does not cancel, bled off by drag, so a sink settles at a steady speed instead of accelerating. A swim built on noclip is a player who can leave a pool through its wall. **Red/Green TDD: 14 fog-node tests + 5 against a real driver, 5 mode tests, 21 swim tests.** Docs: [docs/pbr.html](../docs/pbr.html) §Fog and [docs/navigation.html](../docs/navigation.html). First customer: twig-bb's `twig_bb.underwater`
