<a name="rebinding-a-node-s-children-notifies-nobody"></a>
### Rebinding a node's `children` notifies nobody — ⬜ Todo

`node.children = [child]` is the natural way to write a scenegraph change and it **silently fails to reach the renderer**. `FlatPass` walks the scenegraph once (`SGObserver`) and thereafter keeps its set of renderable paths current from the `OList.NEW_CHILD_EVT` / `DEL_CHILD_EVT` signals a node's `children` list sends as it is added to and removed from. Assigning a *new* list to the field replaces the observed one instead of mutating it, so no signal is sent and the pass goes on drawing whatever it last integrated: the scenegraph is correct, the screen is stale, and nothing anywhere reports a problem. `children[:] = [child]` is the spelling that works, which is a distinction no caller should have to know. **Confirmed in the wild** — it is what made twig-bb draw every weapon with the model of whichever one was held when the level loaded (that project's B32), invisible until somebody switched weapon at *runtime*, since a scene integrated after the change looks right. **Three call sites in this package have the same shape**: `physics/debugdraw.py` rebinds `_proxy_group.children` when new proxies appear, `scenegraph/tilesterrain.py` rebinds `self.children` as tiles stream in, and `bin/gltf_demo.py` rebinds a layer's — each drawing a stale set until something else happens to re-integrate. Wanted: fix it **where the field is set**, so the obvious spelling is the correct one — an `MFNode` set that mutates the existing `OList` in place (and so emits add/remove per element) rather than binding a new list, in pyvrml97's field machinery. That is shared 28-year-old code, so it wants its own Red/Green pass over what currently depends on identity-of-list rather than contents, plus a decision on whether the same rule should hold for every `MF*` field or only for the node-valued ones. The **other half of this defect is already fixed** (2026-08-03): `NodePath.invalidate` was marking only children and grandchildren broken because `iterdescendents` walked two levels against its own docstring, so a detached subtree deeper than that stayed in the draw set; `iterdescendents` now walks every generation and pyvrml97's own test, which had pinned the two-level behaviour, is corrected. Verified against this package's 4,703 unit tests

<a name="navigation-mesh"></a>
### Navigation mesh — 🟡 Built, not yet usable on a level

`OpenGLContext.nav.navmesh`: a navmesh **generated from the collision mesh at load time**, never baked and never read from content — which is the licensing answer and also the better engineering one, since it gives navigation for levels nobody ever baked, regenerates when geometry changes, and depends on nothing we may not read. Walkable triangles by slope and facing; neighbours by shared edge, **welded by position** because a collision mesh from a level is a triangle soup where the same corner has a different index in every triangle that touches it — matching indices joins nothing and leaves every cell an island; A* over the cells and a **string-pulled** path through the portals, because a path along cell centres zigzags across an empty room and every corner it rounds is one it did not need. 25 tests, no GL: an open floor gives a straight line, an obstacle is routed round, a ramp is climbed, an island is unreachable. **On `oa_dm1` it builds 1220 connected cells in 0.03 s and then fails at the last step — no spawn point resolves to a cell** (`cell_at`, and the coordinates line up, so it is the point-in-triangle test or the slope filter). The headroom test is too blunt for a real level and is off by default: it compares bounding boxes where it wants a distance, and removed 1092 of 1220 cells almost none of which were near a wall. No off-mesh links yet, so jump pads are not edges. Next steps are in twig-bb's PROJECT-PLAN §3b T2

**2026-08-29 — the route is now the route a person would take.** The demo took a
visibly wrong line: out of the left room, straight at the far wall, then up the
wall to find the gap. Three things were wrong, and all three are engine defects
rather than demo ones.

- **The funnel worked out each portal's left and right end from its own moving
  apex.** Left and right are a fact about the direction a portal is *crossed*,
  not about where the walker currently stands, so a portal reversed whenever the
  apex passed to the other side of it and the funnel narrowed the wrong side of
  itself. On an open floor a route across the grain of the triangulation gained
  a corner behind the start; handed a corridor that doubled back, it returned a
  line straight through the walls. Portals are now oriented once, per direction,
  in `_join`, and both the funnel and the new raycast read them as stored.
- **A\* costed a step between cell centres**, which over a grid of triangles is
  a Manhattan metric: a staircase costs the same as the two sides it climbs, so
  every monotone corridor tied and the winner was whichever the queue reached
  first. Since the funnel cannot leave the corridor it is handed, that tie was
  the route. A step is now costed from where the walker stands to the **nearest
  point on the portal it leaves by**, with the search state carrying the portal
  a cell was entered through; the diagonal is then strictly cheaper, which is
  what it is. Portal *midpoints* were tried first and are not enough — they are
  exact along the mesh's own diagonal and √2 long across it.
- **Nothing removed a corner the corridor rather than the geometry put there.**
  `NavMesh.visible()` walks the mesh along a line and answers whether an
  unbroken run of cells covers it — a public API a bot wants anyway — and the
  pulled line now keeps only the furthest corner still in sight of the last.

On the demo's geometry the main route falls from 37.30 m to 33.34 m, which is
the taut line: six points, four of them the ends of the two walls. `path()` also
went from 22 ms to 2.5 ms on that mesh — `cell_at` now narrows by footprint box
before testing cells, `_height_at` reads a precomputed normal, and the funnel
and search run on plain floats rather than allocating numpy arrays per portal.
`NavMesh.corridor()` is public, so the demo draws the engine's own search
instead of the copy of A\* it used to carry. 43 tests.

### [LOOP-INSTRUMENTATION.md](LOOP-INSTRUMENTATION.md) — ✅ Complete

Measuring the thing the frame rate is not. **A game can report 50 fps while moving once a second, with every number on screen correct**: `FrameCounter` times only the inside of `OnDraw` (so `poll_events`, `OnIdle` — where an application’s whole simulation usually lives — and the redraw wait are invisible), it is only told about frames that produced a *visible change*, and the rate it publishes is a **median**, deliberately outlier-proof so a shader compile cannot drag it down for ever. Each choice right; together, blind to a stutter. `looptrace.LoopTrace` measures **wall-clock time per loop iteration divided among named phases**, where **a phase is charged only its own time and a nested phase’s belongs to the child** — so the phases of an iteration *sum* to its wall clock and the largest is the culprit by construction, not by comparison. Keeps what the median throws away: `worst_ms`, a `stalls` count that outlives the window it happened in, and the phase breakdown of the last stall. New **Loop** section sits directly under Frame because the two are read *against each other* — a healthy `fps` over a poor `loop fps` is the whole diagnosis. `GLFWContext.MainLoop` opens `poll`/`repeats`/`idle`/`wait`/`draw`; `OnDraw` subdivides into `cascade`/`render`; `Context.tracePhase()` answers a do-nothing manager so callers write the `with` and not the branch; a backend that never opens an iteration gets no section rather than rows of zeroes. Also: `ThreadedSimulation.rate()`/`.dropped` + `simulation_provider` (**a starved physics thread and wrong gravity look identical on screen** until `sim hz` sits beside `asked`), and twig-bb’s `FrameClock`, which keeps the accounting the timestep clamp destroys — `real`, `lost`, cumulative `debt` and `pace`, so “5% speed, 0.9s lost” says outright that the world is running at a twentieth of speed. `OPENGLCONTEXT_STALL_MS` / `OPENGLCONTEXT_TRACE_STALLS` log a stall’s breakdown; counting is always on and costs a few clock reads. **Red/Green TDD: 65 tests**, the load-bearing one being 30 frames at 20ms + one at a second + 30 more — median 20ms, worst 1000ms. Docs: [docs/hud.html](../docs/hud.html)

<a name="animated-pbr-surfaces"></a>
### Animated PBR surfaces — ✅ Complete

A **surface deformer** on `PBRMesh`: a per-frame callable a *material* uses to move the geometry it is painted on, as distinct from morph targets and skinning, which are the mesh's own animation. It rides the same pipeline — rest arrays, the morph→skin→surface chain, the version counter, the dynamic VBO re-upload — and adds the two properties that make it safe: the deformer always sees the **rest pose** (deforming its own last output compounds, and a one-unit wave walks the surface away in seconds) and is handed **copies** (so a deformer that writes in place cannot corrupt what it is given next frame). Texture coordinates join the dynamic buffers **only** when the mesh says its UVs move (`deforms_texcoords`), so a skinned character does not pay to re-upload UVs that never change. Together with the `uv_transform`/`texCoordMask` and material-version seams that already existed for `KHR_texture_transform`, this is what lets Quake 3 `.shader` animation — `tcMod`, `rgbGen`, `alphaGen`, `animMap`, `deformVertexes` — be drawn: see [twig-bb](../../twig-bb/README.md#surfaces-that-move), whose `animator.py` is the worked example. **Red/Green TDD: 22 tests**, no GL. Docs: [docs/pbr.html#animated-surfaces](../docs/pbr.html)

<a name="spatial-audio"></a>
### [SPATIAL-AUDIO.md](SPATIAL-AUDIO.md) — ✅ Complete

Sound placed **in** the scene, on glTF's `KHR_audio_emitter` model (the Web Audio `PannerNode` model) rather than a private format — the same decision physics made with OMI, so a scene authored in Blender or Godot arrives with its sound intact. The engine ships as the standalone **`omi_audio`** package (model/spatial/clip/synth/mixer/device/engine — numpy only, no renderer in it); OpenGLContext keeps the integrations: `scenegraph/audio.py` (`AudioEmitter`/`AudioSource`, and VRML97's long-declared `Sound`/`AudioClip` finally audible on their own ellipsoid curve) and `OpenGLContext/audio/` (scene/settings). <a name="fog-and-swimming"></a>
### Fog, and swimming — ✅ Complete

Two nodes that had been declared and never driven, finally driven. **VRML97's `Fog`** (`scenegraph/fog.py`) is rendered through the PBR shader: the pass has collected `nodetypes.Fog` paths for years and nothing read them, and a dangling `context.gltf_fog` seam that nothing ever set is retired in its favour. A node rather than a number *because fog is a property of a place* — it binds and stacks like a viewpoint, so being under water, in smoke and in clear air are three fogs in one scene with one in force. **Both of the specification's curves**, because they are not approximations of each other: `LINEAR` fades from the first metre, `EXPONENTIAL` hangs back and closes in, and the difference is whether the weapon in the player's hands is tinted as much as the far wall — which is exactly what separates *being inside a medium* from a coloured pane laid over the screen. One uniform (the reciprocal of the visible range) serves every mode, the blend happens in linear HDR before tone mapping, and a scene with no fog stays pixel-identical. **Swimming** stops being a use of noclip: `MovementMode.applyTo(platform)` is where a mode puts the *body* into the state its movement assumes, `SwimMode` asks for `set_swim(buoyancy=...)` rather than `set_fly(True)`, and omi_physics' `CharacterController` grew a swim step — flying's movement, walking's collision, and a vertical that is neither: gravity scaled by what buoyancy does not cancel, bled off by drag, so a sink settles at a steady speed instead of accelerating. A swim built on noclip is a player who can leave a pool through its wall. **Red/Green TDD: 14 fog-node tests + 5 against a real driver, 5 mode tests, 21 swim tests.** Docs: [docs/pbr.html](../docs/pbr.html) §Fog and [docs/navigation.html](../docs/navigation.html).
